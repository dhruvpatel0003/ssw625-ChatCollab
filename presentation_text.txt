--- SLIDE 17 ---
Our Critical Analysis: Why the Paper Matters — and What 
Comes Next
Strengths
• Treats AI as a teammate, not only a coding tool
• Event-driven architecture supports genuine waiting 
and autonomy
• Measures collaboration, not just final code
• Role prompting produces observable behavioral 
differences
• Public code/data improves reproducibility
What we would test next
• Larger and more diverse software tasks
• More human participants and team structures
• Cost, latency, and productivity trade-offs
• Human-aware vs human-agnostic agents
• Failure recovery, safety, and accountability

Takeaway
ChatCollab asks a bigger question than
“Can AI write code?”
Can humans and autonomous AI agents form a functioning 
software team with meaningful roles, coordination, and 
measurable collaboration?
The paper’s initial evidence says: promising — but larger human studies, more complex 
tasks, and efficiency improvements are still needed.
